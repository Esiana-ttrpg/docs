# Campaign Hub

Before you have a campaign, you have a shelf of possible campaigns — the ones you run, the ones you play in, the one you have been meaning to start. The campaign hub is that shelf: your personal home page for resuming play, noticing what needs you, and starting something new. It is deliberately not a social feed. Nobody performs here; you just pick up where you left off.

## How it works

The hub organizes itself around return visits. Pinned campaigns and high-priority tables lead, because those are what you open nine times out of ten; everything you belong to follows in a stable order. A "needs attention" area collects actionable items across all your campaigns — an unread recap, a pending RSVP, an open thread, downtime awaiting review — so one glance tells you whether any table needs you before game night.

Creating a campaign starts here too, through a setup wizard. You give it a name, describe it, pick its game system and language, decide how visible it is, and optionally import existing material — an Obsidian vault, a Kanka export, or a prior Esiana backup — instead of starting from a blank wiki. The wizard also handles the calendar import for worlds with custom time. Import runs in the background after creation; the campaign is yours immediately, and the lore arrives shortly after.

Campaigns live at short shareable addresses, and joining happens two ways. Public campaigns seeking players list themselves in recruitment, where strangers apply with an introduction the GM reviews. Private tables run on invite links: the GM generates a link or emails it directly, and whoever holds it can join without any public listing. Either way, joining makes you a member with a role — owner, GM, writer, player, observer — and membership is what unlocks the campaign's insides.

## Using the hub

For everyday use there is almost nothing to learn: open the hub, see what needs attention, enter the campaign that does. Two habits make it better. Pin the campaigns you actually open weekly so the hub's ordering matches your life. And when something needs you — an RSVP, a recap, an application to review — handle it from the attention list rather than hunting through each campaign; that is what the list is for.

Starting a campaign deserves more care, since early choices echo:

1. From the hub, create a campaign and work through the wizard: name it well (names become addresses and are hard to change gracefully), describe the premise, and pick the game system.
2. Decide visibility now: a private table stays invite-only; a public one can later recruit.
3. If you have existing lore, import it at creation rather than afterward — Obsidian vault, Kanka export, or Esiana backup, plus a Fantasy-Calendar file if your world has custom time. Read the import guide's folder-mapping notes first; ten minutes there saves an hour of refiling.
4. Finish the identity and access steps, create the campaign, and let the import land. Then invite your table.

Joining someone else's campaign is the mirror image: follow an invite link for a private table, or browse recruitment, read the listing honestly, and apply with a real introduction. Guests without accounts can browse public listings and public campaign pages where enabled, but membership — and therefore play — requires an account.

## Campaign configuration

The hub itself has no settings; it reflects your memberships. What it shows is governed by campaign-level choices made elsewhere: visibility and recruitment settings decide which campaigns appear publicly, pinning is personal to you, and the attention list derives from activity in each campaign. Game system tagging deserves a note — it is descriptive metadata (which game you play), not a rules engine. Esiana does not change its behavior for different systems; per-system templates are a future direction, not a current one.

## Administration

Instance administrators see every campaign from the admin area, where they can back up or remove any of them. They also control whether registration is open at all and whether it is restricted to certain email domains — on a closed instance, the hub's audience is whoever the admin admits, and invite links become the primary way tables form.

## Players

Your hub is your table-of-contents for every game you are in. Keep pins current, watch the attention list before sessions, and treat recruitment listings as reading material first: the premise, schedule, table expectations, and safety notes tell you whether a table fits before you spend anyone's time applying.

## Things to know

Campaign addresses derive from names, so a joke name at creation becomes a joke address indefinitely — renaming later regenerates the address and breaks old links. Imports are much easier at creation than after; a running campaign can still restore backups, but vault mapping belongs in the wizard. And invite links are bearer credentials in the practical sense: anyone holding one can join, so rotate the link if it leaks beyond its intended recipients.
