# Campaign Home & Sidebar

Every campaign has two front doors. The wiki is the default entry — the codex the table lives in. Campaign Home is the second: a continuity overview that answers "what is happening and what is next" at a glance, with the session schedule, the world date, open quests, recent lore, and house notices arranged as widgets. The sidebar is the navigation both doors share, and it can be shaped to match how your table actually plays.

## How it works

Campaign Home is a grid of widgets the GM arranges: session schedule and RSVP status, the world chronometer showing the in-world date, the campaign bulletin for pinned house rules and notices, the quest ledger of active quests, recent lore changes, the fantasy calendar snapshot, living threads, and more. A hero section with a cover image and summary can frame the page. Widgets show role-appropriate content automatically — players see the party's view of quests and threads, never the GM's hidden state.

Up to ten widgets can be active; a half-dozen well-chosen ones serve most tables better than a full grid. The point of the page is triage, not completeness: a player opening it five minutes before the session should learn when the game is, where the story stands, and what changed since last time.

The sidebar holds the campaign's navigation in two groups — world lore (wiki, maps, chronology, and the like) and game management (sessions, downtime, journals, settings) — with reorderable sections and customizable headings. Renaming "Game Management" to "Table Business" is exactly the kind of small localization that makes a campaign feel owned. Recent changes gets its own feed: a chronological activity list of what was edited, useful both as a GM audit trail and as a player asking "did anything move since Tuesday?"

## Using Campaign Home and the sidebar

As GM, treat Campaign Home as a page you curate, not a page you fill. Early in a campaign, enable the widgets that answer your table's recurring questions — almost always the session schedule, the quest ledger, and recent lore — write a one-paragraph summary in the hero, and pin the two or three house rules players actually forget. Revisit the layout when the campaign's phase changes: a travel arc wants the chronometer and maps prominent; a political arc wants threads and factions.

Sidebar shaping follows the same instinct. Open Campaign Settings → Sidebar, reorder sections so the things your table opens daily sit high, and rename headings to your table's vocabulary. The wiki's folder structure does the heavy organizing; the sidebar decides what is one click away.

Players mostly consume both surfaces: read the bulletin, check the schedule, RSVP from the schedule widget or the session page, skim recent changes before the game. Nothing here requires instruction beyond existing — if a widget confuses your players, that is feedback about the layout, and the GM should simplify it.

## Campaign configuration

Campaign Home layout editing and sidebar order are GM and writer privileges, configured in the customize mode on Campaign Home itself and in Campaign Settings → Sidebar. Theme choices — preset and appearance profile — frame both surfaces and can override personal themes when the campaign asserts its look. See Campaign Settings for the full catalog; the Home-and-sidebar-relevant pieces are layout, sidebar order and headings, and theme.

## Players

You will spend more time on Campaign Home than anywhere except the wiki, so learn its three moves: check the schedule, read the bulletin, skim what changed. RSVP from wherever is convenient — the widget and the session page are the same action. If the page feels cluttered, say so; layout is the GM's job, but signal from players is how it gets better.

## Things to know

Campaign Home is an overview, not an archive: widgets summarize and link, and the underlying pages remain the source of truth. Hiding a widget hides a summary, never the content. Sidebar order is navigation, not permission — moving Sessions below Maps changes no one's access. And the default entry for a campaign is the wiki, so deep-linking players to Campaign Home for session-zero reading is a kindness the system will not do automatically.
