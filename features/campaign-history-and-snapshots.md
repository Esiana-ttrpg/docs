# Campaign History & Snapshots

Campaign worlds change. Factions rise and fall, NPCs move, rumors spread, danger shifts — and six months later nobody remembers exactly what the region looked like when the party first arrived. Campaign history is Esiana's answer: named, frozen pictures of the world at meaningful moments, which you can later hold side by side and compare.

Start with the intuition, because the terminology follows from it. A snapshot is a preserved picture of the campaign world as it stood at one moment — who held what, who was where, what the party knew. Snapshots are not save files you restore, not alternate timelines, and not fiction. They are reference points: "the Marches as of the end of Act I." Comparing two snapshots answers "what changed between then and now" the way a diff answers it for text.

## How it works

Snapshots come in two flavors, and the distinction matters because only some of them can be compared.

Visit snapshots happen on their own. Whenever you mark a location as visited by the party, Esiana freezes the region's state at that moment — the fact of the visit becomes a baseline for next time. This is what powers "since your last visit" summaries: the system already kept the receipt.

Milestones are the ones you create deliberately, from Campaign History at the bottom of the Chronology feed. Name them for the moment they capture — "End of the Ash War," "Before the three-year skip" — optionally anchored to a location. A milestone without a location anchor captures the campaign in general; one anchored to Winterfort captures Winterfort in particular. Only anchored, fully-detailed snapshots can be compared region-to-region, which is worth knowing before you build a ritual around unanchored ones.

Comparison always runs older-to-newer (the interface corrects a reversed selection rather than scolding you) and summarizes regional change: faction stance shifts, NPC presence and movement, rumor and party-knowledge deltas, danger changes, location evolution. What you see depends on who you are: GMs get the full operational diff, player-facing surfaces get the party's view only. Two saved moments are required — comparing against the live, still-moving present is not available — and archived snapshots, kept for history, can no longer be compared.

Good milestone moments practically name themselves: the end of a story arc, either side of a time skip, the start or end of a war, a major discovery or revelation, a decisive shift in faction power. Visit snapshots cover routine travel, so save named milestones for the moments the campaign will be remembered by.

## Using campaign history

Open Chronology, then the feed, then Campaign History at the bottom. The workflow is short:

1. At a moment worth remembering, create a milestone: name it, anchor it to the location it is about, and save. It freezes the world as of the current campaign date.
2. Let the campaign move on. Visits accumulate their own automatic baselines in the meantime.
3. When you want the comparison — prepping the "five years later" session, writing the arc recap, answering "who rules Elderhelm now?" — pick the earlier and later snapshots and compare.
4. Read the diff as prep, not prose. It tells you what changed; the recap you write for the players is still yours to write.

## Campaign configuration

Campaign history has no settings of its own. Who can create milestones follows the campaign's management permissions — this is a GM and Co-DM surface. The quality of comparisons follows from habits elsewhere: anchored locations, dated events, and visited marks are what give the diff something to chew on.

## Players

Players do not operate snapshots, but they benefit from them twice over. "Since your last visit" summaries — what changed in this region while you were away — are built on visit snapshots, and arc recaps the GM writes after comparing milestones tend to be sharper than ones written from memory. If your GM shares a comparison with you, remember you are seeing the party's view: the full picture stays behind the screen.

## Things to know

Snapshots freeze the world; they do not freeze your notes, and editing a wiki page today does not rewrite a snapshot from last month — that immutability is the point. Comparisons need two fully-detailed, anchored moments; an unanchored campaign-wide milestone and an archived snapshot will both refuse with an explanation rather than a broken diff. And because comparison is region-to-region, the most useful milestones are anchored to the places your campaign actually returns to.
