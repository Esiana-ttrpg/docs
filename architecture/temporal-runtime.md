# Temporal Runtime

Time in Esiana is a single shared now with many readers. The campaign clock says what day it is on the world's own calendar; every dated surface — the timeline, the map, gated lore, quest pressures, scheduled downtime — renders itself as of that moment. Move the now and the world re-renders. That sentence, taken seriously, explains most of this page.

The clock itself is simple: a current date on the master calendar, advanced deliberately by whoever manages chronology. Months have real lengths, moons have real cycles, festivals fall where the calendar says they do, so "three weeks later" lands on a date the world's own logic honors. Parallel calendars are possible but rarely needed; most campaigns live on one clock, and the master calendar is protected accordingly.

What makes time a *runtime* rather than a label is that so many systems read the same now. Timeline events sort and expand around it. Maps draw the world as of it. Gated lore unlocks against it. Quest pressures tick with it. Scheduled downtime fires on it. None of these keep their own time; all resolve the shared now through their own rules, which is why the surfaces can honestly disagree in presentation — a map shows geography-as-of, a rumor feed shows knowledge-as-of — while never disagreeing about what day it is.

Two consequences follow that GMs use constantly. First, time travel is free: set the viewing date and the world renders historically, which is how flashback prep, anniversary browsing, and "what did we know then" all work without special features. Second, the clock and the world are separate verbs: stepping the date changes the now, while world advance simulates what the world did with the elapsed time. Skip the second and the date moves through an unchanged world; skip the first and effects have no when to land on. The five quiet years between arcs want both.

Snapshots freeze moments against this runtime: visit baselines accumulate on their own as the party travels, and named milestones preserve the moments worth comparing. Since every snapshot is dated against the same clock, comparisons always run older-to-newer over shared ground.

Feature guides for the hands-on parts: chronology and calendars for operating the clock, world advance for simulating elapsed time, campaign history for freezing and comparing moments.
